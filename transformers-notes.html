§ 01

Why attention at all

▸ Bahdanau et al. 2014 — Attention for NMT ▸ Vaswani et al. 2017 — Attention Is All You Need

Before 2017, the standard way to model a sequence was a recurrent network: read one token, update a hidden state, repeat. That design has three structural problems, and the Transformer is best understood as a direct answer to all three at once.

Problem with RNNsConsequenceTransformer's answer
Computation is inherently sequential in n Cannot use a GPU's parallelism during training All positions attend simultaneously — one matrix multiply
Path length between token i and token j is |i−j| steps Gradients vanish over long range; long-distance agreement is hard Path length is O(1) — every token reads every other token directly
Fixed-size hidden state must compress all history Information bottleneck; early tokens get overwritten No compression — the whole prefix stays addressable as keys and values
Intuition

An RNN is a person reading a book while allowed to keep only one sticky note, rewritten after every sentence. Attention is the same person allowed to keep the whole book open and flip back to any page — but they must decide, for each new word they write, which pages to look at. Attention is the mechanism that makes "which pages to look at" differentiable and therefore learnable.

The trade we accept

We buy O(1) path length by paying O(n²) compute: every token compares itself to every other token. That quadratic term is the single most consequential design decision in modern AI — it is why context windows are expensive, why FlashAttention exists, why KV caches dominate serving memory, and why state-space models (Module 2) are interesting at all. Keep it in view.

The original architecture vs. what we actually build today

The 2017 paper describes an encoder–decoder model for machine translation: an encoder with bidirectional self-attention, a decoder with masked self-attention plus cross-attention into the encoder. Almost every large language model you will use in this course — GPT, LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, Claude — is a decoder-only model: it keeps the masked self-attention and the feed-forward network, and drops the encoder and the cross-attention entirely. These notes teach the decoder-only stack as the primary object, and mention the encoder only for contrast (§15).

§ 02

The language-modelling objective

▸ Radford et al. 2018 — GPT-1 ▸ Brown et al. 2020 — GPT-3

Everything the architecture does is in service of one objective. A decoder-only Transformer is a machine for estimating the probability of a token sequence, factorised left to right by the chain rule of probability:

$$P_\theta(x_1,\dots,x_n)=\prod_{t=1}^{n}P_\theta\!\left(x_t \mid x_{<t}\right)$$

The model is trained by maximum likelihood, which for a categorical distribution is exactly cross-entropy. For a single sequence:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\log P_\theta\!\left(x_t \mid x_{<t}\right) = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\log \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(z_t\right)_{x_t}$$

where $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ is the logit vector the model emits at position t. Two consequences follow immediately, and they explain the entire design of causal attention:

  1. Position t must not see token t+1. If it could, the task would be trivially solvable by copying, and the model would learn nothing. This is the sole reason the causal mask exists (§7).
  2. Every position is a training example. One sequence of length n gives n supervised predictions, all computable in a single forward pass. This is teacher forcing, and it is why Transformer training parallelises so well.
Perplexity

Perplexity is the exponentiated mean cross-entropy, $\mathrm{PPL}=\exp(\mathcal{L})$. Read it as "the model is as confused as if it were choosing uniformly among PPL equally likely tokens." A uniform model over a 50,000-token vocabulary has PPL = 50,000. A good modern LLM on English web text sits in the single digits to low teens. Because PPL depends on the tokenizer, perplexities across different tokenizers are not comparable — a classic exam trap.

Worked example — one training sequence

Input ["The","cat","sat","on"], targets ["cat","sat","on","the"]. The batch tensor is simply the sequence shifted by one. Loss is averaged over 4 predictions. Position 0 sees only The; position 3 sees all four. One forward pass, four gradients — no loop.

§ 03

Tokens, embeddings, and the residual stream

▸ Sennrich et al. 2015 — BPE ▸ Kudo & Richardson 2018 — SentencePiece ▸ Press & Wolf 2016 — Weight tying

Tokenisation

Text is first cut into subword units by byte-pair encoding (BPE) or a variant. BPE starts from bytes and repeatedly merges the most frequent adjacent pair, producing a vocabulary V of typically 32k–256k entries. Frequent words become single tokens (" the"); rare words fragment ("tokenization" → "token" + "ization"). Nothing is out-of-vocabulary because the base alphabet is all 256 bytes.

Why tokenisation is not a footnote

A surprising number of LLM failure modes are tokenisation artefacts, not reasoning failures: poor arithmetic (numbers split inconsistently, e.g. 1234 → 12+34), inability to count letters in a word (the model never sees letters), and weak performance on low-resource languages (which fragment into many more tokens, consuming context and compute). Before blaming the architecture, check the tokenizer.

Embedding

Each token id is a row lookup in an embedding matrix $W_E \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times d_{\text{model}}}$. For a sequence of n ids we obtain $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_{\text{model}}}$. Throughout these notes $d \equiv d_{\text{model}}$ — 768 for GPT-2 small, 4096 for LLaMA-2-7B.

At the output, logits are produced by an unembedding matrix $W_U \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times |V|}$. Many models set $W_U = W_E^{\top}$ (weight tying), saving $|V| \cdot d$ parameters — 38M for GPT-2 small, a third of the whole model — and usually improving perplexity.

The residual stream — the mental model that makes everything else click

Do not think of a Transformer as a pipeline that transforms X into something else at each layer. Think of it as a shared bus of width d, one lane per token position, running the full depth of the network. Every sub-layer reads a linear projection of the bus, computes something, and adds its result back:

$$x \leftarrow x + \text{Attn}(\text{norm}(x)), \qquad x \leftarrow x + \text{MLP}(\text{norm}(x))$$

Because every operation is additive, the final representation is literally the sum of the embedding plus every head's and every MLP's contribution. This is what makes gradients flow to depth 100, and it is the foundation of mechanistic interpretability (§16). Attention moves information between lanes; MLPs process information within a lane. That one sentence is most of what a Transformer block does.

§ 04

Position: the missing ingredient

▸ Sinusoidal — Vaswani 2017 §3.5 ▸ Su et al. 2021 — RoPE ▸ Press et al. 2021 — ALiBi ▸ Shaw et al. 2018 — Relative PE

Self-attention is a set operation. Permute the rows of X and the output rows permute identically — the mechanism is permutation equivariant. On its own it cannot distinguish "dog bites man" from "man bites dog". Position must be injected explicitly.

Proof sketch

Let P be a permutation matrix. Since $Q=XW_Q$ etc. are row-wise linear, $(PX)W_Q = P(XW_Q)$. The score matrix becomes $PQK^\top P^\top$, softmax is applied row-wise so it commutes with P, and the output is $P\,\mathrm{Attn}(X)$. Order information is genuinely absent, not merely weakened. (Note: with a causal mask, permutation equivariance breaks — the mask itself encodes ordering — but the model still cannot measure distance, which is what positional encodings supply.)

Three families

1. Sinusoidal (absolute, added)

$$PE_{(t,2i)}=\sin\!\left(\frac{t}{10000^{2i/d}}\right),\qquad PE_{(t,2i+1)}=\cos\!\left(\frac{t}{10000^{2i/d}}\right)$$

Each dimension pair is a clock hand rotating at a different frequency — a binary-counter representation in continuous form. Its useful property: $PE_{t+k}$ is a fixed linear function of $PE_t$ (a 2×2 rotation by $k\omega_i$), so relative offsets are linearly decodable. Added to the embedding, not concatenated.

2. Learned absolute

A trainable table $W_P \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{\max}\times d}$ (GPT-1, GPT-2, BERT). Simple and effective, but it cannot extrapolate past $n_{\max}$ — there is no row to look up.

3. RoPE — rotary position embedding (the modern default)

Used by LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek, PaLM. Instead of adding anything to the embedding, RoPE rotates the query and key vectors by an angle proportional to position, in each 2-D coordinate pair. For pair i with frequency $\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d_h}$:

$$\begin{pmatrix}\tilde q^{(i)}_1\\ \tilde q^{(i)}_2\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}\cos m\theta_i & -\sin m\theta_i\\ \sin m\theta_i & \cos m\theta_i\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}q^{(i)}_1\\ q^{(i)}_2\end{pmatrix} \;\equiv\; R_{m,i}\,q^{(i)}$$

The magic is what happens in the dot product. Since rotation matrices satisfy $R_m^\top R_n = R_{n-m}$:

$$\langle \tilde q_m, \tilde k_n\rangle = q_m^\top R_m^\top R_n k_n = q_m^\top R_{n-m}\, k_n$$
Why RoPE won

The attention score depends only on the relative offset n−m, even though we only ever applied an absolute rotation to each vector individually. You get relative-position behaviour at absolute-position cost — no extra parameters, no $O(n^2)$ bias matrix, and it composes cleanly with the KV cache (rotate keys once, when you cache them). It also degrades gracefully, which is why context-extension tricks (position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling, YaRN) all operate by rescaling $\theta_i$.

4. ALiBi — no embedding at all

Add a linear penalty to the pre-softmax scores: $s_{ij} \mathrel{+}= -m_h\,(i-j)$, with a head-specific slope $m_h$. Attention decays with distance by construction. Extremely simple and extrapolates well past the training length.

§ 05

Self-attention from first principles

▸ Vaswani et al. 2017 §3.2 ▸ The Annotated Transformer ▸ Phuong & Hutter 2022 — Formal Algorithms

Rather than presenting the formula and explaining it afterwards, let us derive it. The question self-attention answers is: for each token, which other tokens should shape its representation, and by how much?

Step 1 — a differentiable dictionary

A Python dictionary d[query] does a hard lookup: exact key match, return one value. We want a soft version — compare the query against all keys, and return a weighted average of all values, weighted by match quality. Written out:

$$\text{out}(q) = \sum_j \underbrace{\frac{\exp\!\big(\text{sim}(q,k_j)\big)}{\sum_{j'}\exp\!\big(\text{sim}(q,k_{j'})\big)}}_{\text{attention weight } \alpha_j} \; v_j$$

The softmax turns arbitrary similarity scores into a probability distribution over positions — non-negative, summing to 1 — and it is smooth, so gradients flow into both the values and the decision of where to look.

Step 2 — where Q, K, V come from

In self-attention, queries, keys and values are all linear projections of the same input X. Three learned matrices give the model three different "views" of each token:

$$Q = XW_Q,\qquad K = XW_K,\qquad V = XW_V$$ $$W_Q,W_K \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d_k},\qquad W_V \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d_v}$$
What each projection means — the classified-ads metaphor
  • Query $q_i$ — what token i is looking for. "I am a verb; I need my subject."
  • Key $k_j$ — what token j advertises. "I am a plural noun in subject position."
  • Value $v_j$ — what token j hands over if selected. "Here is my number and animacy information."

Keys and queries decide routing; values carry content. That separation is the point: the same token can be highly attended-to for one reason while contributing entirely different information. This is exactly the QK-circuit / OV-circuit split of §16.

Step 3 — the similarity function

The paper uses a scaled dot product. Two candidates were considered:

  • Additive (Bahdanau): $\text{sim}(q,k)=w^\top\tanh(W_1q+W_2k)$. A small MLP per pair — expressive but slow.
  • Dot-product (chosen): $\text{sim}(q,k)=q^\top k$. No parameters, and the whole $n\times n$ score matrix is one matmul $QK^\top$ — perfectly suited to GPU tensor cores.

Dot product is not less expressive: $W_Q$ and $W_K$ already supply the learnable part, since $q_i^\top k_j = x_i^\top (W_Q W_K^\top) x_j$ is a full learned bilinear form on the inputs.

Step 4 — the formula

$$\boxed{\;\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V\;}$$

Row i of A is "how much token i attends to each token". Rows sum to 1; columns do not. A column summing to a large value means that token is broadcasting to many others.

Concrete: "The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired."

At the position of it, the query encodes "I am a pronoun; I need my antecedent — a singular animate noun." The key at animal matches strongly; the key at street matches weakly. So $\alpha_{\text{animal}} \approx 0.6$, $\alpha_{\text{street}} \approx 0.05$, and the output at it is dominated by $v_{\text{animal}}$ — the representation of it literally absorbs animal-ness. Change tired to wide and a well-trained model flips the attention to street. That flip is coreference resolution learned with no coreference supervision.

Cross-attention, for contrast

Identical formula, different sources: $Q$ from the decoder, $K,V$ from the encoder. Decoder-only LLMs have no cross-attention; they concatenate the "source" into the same sequence and let causal self-attention handle it. That simplification is why prompting works the way it does.

§ 06

Why divide by √dk

This is the most commonly asked and most commonly hand-waved detail in the paper. Here is the full argument.

Variance derivation

Assume the components of q and k are independent with mean 0 and variance 1 (roughly true at initialisation, and approximately maintained by LayerNorm). Then for $q^\top k=\sum_{i=1}^{d_k}q_i k_i$:

$$\mathbb{E}[q^\top k]=\sum_i \mathbb{E}[q_i]\mathbb{E}[k_i]=0$$ $$\operatorname{Var}(q^\top k)=\sum_{i=1}^{d_k}\operatorname{Var}(q_ik_i)=\sum_{i=1}^{d_k}1 = d_k$$

So the score has standard deviation $\sqrt{d_k}$. With $d_k=64$, typical scores are ±8; with $d_k=128$, ±11. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ restores unit variance and keeps scores O(1) regardless of head width.

Why large scores are fatal

Softmax with large-magnitude inputs saturates: it approaches a one-hot vector. And the Jacobian of softmax is

$$\frac{\partial \alpha_i}{\partial s_j} = \alpha_i(\delta_{ij}-\alpha_j)$$

which is zero when any $\alpha_i \in \{0,1\}$. A saturated softmax passes no gradient at all — the attention pattern freezes at whatever random routing initialisation gave it, and never learns. The $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ factor is a trainability fix, not a numerical-overflow fix.

See it: temperature and softmax sharpness

Same five raw scores, divided by different values of √dk. Watch the distribution collapse to one-hot as the divisor shrinks.


Raw scores: 6.0, 4.5, 2.0, 1.0, −1.5. Small divisor → one-hot → zero gradient.

Exam question

"Softmax is shift-invariant, so why not just subtract the max instead of scaling?" — Subtracting the max fixes overflow (and every implementation does it). It does not change the spread of the scores, so it does not fix saturation. Scaling changes the spread. They solve different problems and both are used.

§ 07

Causal (masked) attention

▸ Vaswani 2017 §3.2.3 — "masked attention" ▸ Child et al. 2019 — Sparse Transformers

The objective in §2 requires that the prediction at position t depend only on $x_{\le t}$. Self-attention as defined lets every position see every other, including the future. We fix this by masking the score matrix before the softmax.

$$M_{ij}=\begin{cases}0 & j\le i \quad(\text{allowed: past and self})\\[2pt] -\infty & j>i \quad(\text{blocked: future})\end{cases}$$ $$A=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}+M\right)$$

Because $e^{-\infty}=0$, masked entries receive exactly zero weight, and — crucially — the surviving entries are renormalised so each row still sums to 1. In code the $-\infty$ is a large negative finite number (-1e9, or torch.finfo(dtype).min) to stay safe in fp16.

The causal mask, live

Hover or tap a row to isolate it. Coloured cells are attendable; grey cells are masked. Note the strictly lower-triangular-plus-diagonal shape.

Row i = query at position i. Column j = key at position j. Row 0 sees only itself; row 7 sees everything.

Three things students get wrong
  1. "Mask after softmax and renormalise." Mathematically it gives the same forward values here, but it wastes compute, breaks fused kernels, and — if you forget to renormalise — silently produces rows that don't sum to 1. Always mask before.
  2. "Set masked weights to 0 by multiplying." Multiplying post-softmax by a 0/1 mask leaves rows summing to less than 1, which shrinks the output magnitude at early positions and destabilises training.
  3. "Causal means position i can't see itself." It can. The diagonal is unmasked. Predicting $x_{t+1}$ from $x_{\le t}$ includes $x_t$.
The asymmetry that defines the architecture

Training is parallel; generation is sequential. During training the whole $n\times n$ masked matrix is computed at once and all n losses are obtained in one pass — the mask is what makes this legitimate. During generation you only ever have the prefix, so you must emit one token, append it, and re-run. The mask guarantees that the parallel training computation is exactly the computation generation will perform, so there is no train/test mismatch in the attention itself.

Note the corollary that trips people up: causal attention is a purely mechanical restriction on the score matrix, not a change of formula. Everything else — Q, K, V, scaling, multi-head, the MLP — is identical to bidirectional attention.

Prefix-LM / document masks

The mask is just a boolean matrix, so you can shape it. A prefix-LM mask is bidirectional over the prompt and causal over the completion (used by UL2, PaLM in some modes). A document mask blocks attention across document boundaries when several documents are packed into one training sequence — cheap, and it prevents the model learning spurious cross-document dependencies. Sliding-window attention (Mistral) masks anything further than w tokens back, making the cost linear in n.

Numeric check — 4 tokens, 1 head

Suppose the row of raw scores for query i=2 is [2.0, 1.0, 3.0, 5.0]. The mask kills index 3 (the future), leaving [2.0, 1.0, 3.0, −∞]. Softmax over the survivors: e²=7.39, e¹=2.72, e³=20.09, sum = 30.20, giving [0.245, 0.090, 0.665, 0.000]. Notice the largest raw score (5.0) was discarded and the remaining weights renormalised upward — that renormalisation is the whole point.

§ 08

Multi-head attention

▸ Vaswani 2017 §3.2.2 ▸ Michel et al. 2019 — Are 16 heads better than one? ▸ Voita et al. 2019 — Head specialisation

The problem with one head

A single attention head produces one distribution per query. But the word bank in "she sat on the bank of the river" needs to attend to sat (syntactic role), to river (disambiguating semantics), and to the (determiner agreement) — simultaneously and for different reasons. One softmax is a bottleneck: it is a convex combination, so putting weight on river necessarily takes weight away from sat.

The fix: run h attentions in parallel on subspaces

$$\text{head}_i=\operatorname{Attention}(XW_Q^{(i)},\,XW_K^{(i)},\,XW_V^{(i)})$$ $$\operatorname{MHA}(X)=\operatorname{Concat}(\text{head}_1,\dots,\text{head}_h)\,W_O$$

with $W_Q^{(i)},W_K^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_k}$, $W_V^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_v}$, $W_O\in\mathbb{R}^{h d_v\times d}$.

The standard sizing convention

Set $d_k=d_v=d/h$. Then the concatenation is again width d, and total FLOPs and parameters are the same as a single head of full width d. Multi-head attention is therefore free relative to single-head — you are not spending more, you are spending the same budget on h narrow, independent lookups instead of one wide one. GPT-2 small: $d=768,\;h=12,\;d_k=64$. LLaMA-2-7B: $d=4096,\;h=32,\;d_k=128$.

Parameter count per MHA layer: $4d^2$ (for $W_Q,W_K,W_V,W_O$), plus biases if used.

WO is not a formality — the per-head decomposition

Split $W_O$ row-wise into h blocks $W_O^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_v\times d}$. Then concat-then-project is exactly a sum:

$$\operatorname{MHA}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\text{head}_i\,W_O^{(i)} =\sum_{i=1}^{h} A^{(i)}\,X\,\underbrace{W_V^{(i)}W_O^{(i)}}_{\text{the OV circuit}}$$

Each head writes its own additive contribution into the residual stream, entirely independently of the others. Heads never interact within a layer — they only interact by both writing to the stream and being read by a later layer. This decomposition is the starting point of A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits and is worth memorising.

What the heads specialise into

Empirically, heads discover interpretable roles with no supervision:

  • Positional / previous-token heads — attend to i−1. Ubiquitous in layer 0–1.
  • Syntactic heads — verb→subject, noun→determiner, preposition→object.
  • Induction heads — the ones that implement in-context learning (§16).
  • Attention sinks — heads that dump most of their mass on token 0 or on delimiters. This is a "no-op": the head has nothing to contribute at this position, and softmax forces it to put its mass somewhere. Recognising this explains a lot of otherwise baffling attention maps.
Common question

"If heads are so redundant that most can be pruned at test time (Michel et al. 2019), why train with many?" — Redundancy at convergence does not imply redundancy during optimisation. Many heads give many parallel lottery tickets for discovering a useful routing pattern; heads that lose become approximately no-ops. Pruning after training is a compression result, not an argument for training narrow.

§ 09

The rest of the block: norm, MLP, residual

▸ Ba et al. 2016 — LayerNorm ▸ Zhang & Sennrich 2019 — RMSNorm ▸ Xiong et al. 2020 — Pre-LN vs Post-LN ▸ Shazeer 2020 — GLU variants (SwiGLU)

Normalisation, and where to put it

LayerNorm standardises each token vector across its d features (not across the batch — that would leak information between sequences and break at batch size 1):

$$\operatorname{LN}(x)=\gamma\odot\frac{x-\mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\epsilon}}+\beta,\qquad \mu=\tfrac1d\textstyle\sum_i x_i$$

RMSNorm drops the mean-centering and the bias — empirically the centering does little, and removing it saves a reduction pass:

$$\operatorname{RMSNorm}(x)=\gamma\odot\frac{x}{\sqrt{\tfrac1d\sum_i x_i^2+\epsilon}}$$

LLaMA, Mistral, Gemma and most modern models use RMSNorm.

Post-LN vs Pre-LN — a bug fix disguised as a hyperparameter

The 2017 paper used Post-LN: $x \leftarrow \operatorname{LN}(x+\text{Sublayer}(x))$. This puts a normalisation on the residual path, so gradients are rescaled at every layer; deep Post-LN models diverge without a careful learning-rate warmup.

Every modern model uses Pre-LN: $x \leftarrow x+\text{Sublayer}(\operatorname{LN}(x))$. Now the residual path is a clean identity from embedding to output — gradients reach layer 1 undistorted, and 100-layer models train stably. Xiong et al. (2020) showed this removes the need for warmup almost entirely. If a deep Transformer you write refuses to train, check this first.

The feed-forward network

A position-wise two-layer MLP, applied identically and independently to every token:

$$\operatorname{FFN}(x)=W_2\,\phi(W_1x+b_1)+b_2,\qquad W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{ff}\times d},\; d_{ff}=4d$$

Activation $\phi$ was ReLU in 2017, then GELU (GPT-2, BERT), and now usually SwiGLU, a gated variant:

$$\operatorname{SwiGLU}(x)=\big(\operatorname{Swish}(W_1x)\odot W_3x\big)W_2$$

SwiGLU uses three matrices instead of two, so implementations shrink $d_{ff}$ to about $\tfrac83 d$ to keep the parameter count matched.

What the MLP is for

Attention is (given the weights) a linear map on values — it mixes information across positions but cannot compute a nonlinear function of it. The MLP supplies the nonlinearity and, with $d_{ff}=4d$, holds roughly two-thirds of all parameters in the model. A productive reading, supported by interpretability work, is key–value memory: $W_1$ rows are pattern detectors, $W_2$ columns are the facts written back when a pattern fires. Attention routes; the MLP knows things.

The complete decoder block

$$\begin{aligned} h &= x + \operatorname{MHA}\big(\operatorname{norm}(x),\ \text{causal mask}\big)\\ y &= h + \operatorname{FFN}\big(\operatorname{norm}(h)\big) \end{aligned}$$

That is the entire block. Stack it L times. GPT-2 small: L=12. GPT-3: L=96. LLaMA-2-70B: L=80.

The full decoder-only stack, end to end

Inside step 4, the attention itself reshapes: B × n × d → B × h × n × d_k so that the $QK^\top$ matmul batches over heads, giving scores of shape B × h × n × n, then merges back to B × n × d before $W_O$.

From logits to text

At generation time only the last position's logits matter. Decoding strategies (Module 4) operate here: greedy (argmax), temperature $\operatorname{softmax}(z/T)$, top-k, top-p/nucleus, beam search. Note that temperature at inference is the same mathematical knob as $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ inside attention — both control how peaked a softmax is. Two different places, one idea.

§ 11

Cost: parameters, FLOPs, memory

▸ Kaplan et al. 2020 — Scaling laws ▸ Hoffmann et al. 2022 — Chinchilla

Parameter count

Per layer, ignoring norms and biases:

$$P_{\text{layer}} = \underbrace{4d^2}_{W_Q,W_K,W_V,W_O} + \underbrace{2\,d\,d_{ff}}_{\text{FFN}} = 4d^2 + 8d^2 = 12d^2 \quad (\text{with } d_{ff}=4d)$$ $$P_{\text{total}} \approx 12\,L\,d^2 + \underbrace{|V|\,d}_{\text{embedding}}$$
Check it on GPT-2 small

$L=12,\ d=768,\ |V|=50257,\ n_{\max}=1024$.
Blocks: $12\times12\times768^2 = 84.9\text{M}$. Embeddings: $50257\times768 = 38.6\text{M}$. Positions: $1024\times768=0.8\text{M}$.
Total ≈ 124M — the published figure. Notice the embedding is 31% of the model; at 7B scale it is under 2%.

FLOPs

Two rules of thumb you should be able to state from memory:

  • Forward pass: $\approx 2N$ FLOPs per token, where N is the non-embedding parameter count (one multiply + one add per weight).
  • Training step: $\approx 6N$ per token — forward (2N) plus backward (4N, since the backward pass computes gradients w.r.t. both inputs and weights).

The attention score computation adds $\approx 4\,L\,n\,d$ per token on top. Setting the two equal shows the crossover point where attention starts to dominate the MLPs: $n \approx 6d$. For $d=4096$ that is around 24k tokens — which is exactly why quadratic attention was tolerable for years and became the bottleneck the moment long context became a product requirement.

The memory wall

A naive implementation materialises the B × h × n × n attention matrix. At B=8, h=32, n=8192, fp16 that is 34 GB for a single layer. This — not the FLOPs — is what actually broke long context, and it is precisely what FlashAttention removes (§13).

§ 12

Inference and the KV cache

Generation is autoregressive: emit one token, append, repeat. Naively, generating token t+1 re-runs the whole model on all t+1 tokens — total cost $O(n^2)$ forward passes' worth of work, most of it recomputation.

Why caching is correct, and why only causal models can do it

Under a causal mask, the key and value vectors at position j depend only on $x_{\le j}$. Appending a new token cannot change any earlier K or V. So cache them. Each new step computes just one query, dots it against t cached keys, and mixes t cached values.

A bidirectional (encoder) model has no such guarantee — every K and V changes when the sequence changes. The KV cache is a direct dividend of causal masking, and one of the strongest practical arguments for decoder-only architectures.

PhaseWhat runsCost per stepBound by
Prefill (prompt)Full parallel pass over np tokens O(n_p²·d)Compute (GPU-saturating)
Decode (each new token)One query vs. cached K/V O(t·d)Memory bandwidth — you must re-read all weights and the whole cache per token

Cache size — know this formula

$$\text{KV bytes} = 2 \times L \times n \times h_{kv} \times d_k \times \text{bytes/elem} \times B$$

(the leading 2 is for K and V).

LLaMA-2-13B, 4096 context, fp16, batch 1

$2 \times 40 \times 4096 \times 40 \times 128 \times 2 = 3.35$ GB. At batch 32 that is 107 GB of cache — larger than the 26 GB of model weights. Serving throughput is a KV-cache-capacity problem, which is exactly what PagedAttention/vLLM (Module 4) and GQA (§13) attack.

§ 13

MQA, GQA, and FlashAttention

Shrinking the cache: MHA → MQA → GQA

All three keep h query heads. They differ in how many key/value heads exist.

SchemeK/V headsCache sizeQualityUsed by
MHAh1× (baseline)BestGPT-2, LLaMA-1
MQA1h× smallerNoticeable degradationPaLM, Falcon
GQAg groups (e.g. 8)h/g× smaller≈ MHALLaMA-2/3 70B, Mistral, Qwen

GQA is the interpolation: query heads are partitioned into g groups, and all heads in a group share one K and one V head. LLaMA-2-70B uses h=64, g=8 — an 8× cache reduction at near-zero quality cost. Ainslie et al. also showed you can "uptrain" an existing MHA checkpoint into GQA by mean-pooling the K/V heads within each group and fine-tuning for a few percent of the original compute.

Why this works at all

What makes heads useful is different queries — different questions asked of the same context. The keys and values (the "index" and the "content") are far more redundant across heads than the queries are. So sharing K/V is the cheapest place to cut. Note that MQA/GQA barely change training FLOPs; they are a decode-time memory-bandwidth optimisation, which is the phase that dominates serving cost.

FlashAttention: same maths, no n×n matrix

FlashAttention is an IO-aware exact algorithm. It is not an approximation — outputs are numerically equivalent (up to floating-point reassociation). The insight is that attention on a GPU is bound by traffic between fast on-chip SRAM (~20 MB, ~19 TB/s) and slow HBM (~80 GB, ~2 TB/s), not by arithmetic.

  1. Tile Q, K, V into blocks that fit in SRAM.
  2. For each Q block, loop over K/V blocks, computing partial attention on chip.
  3. Combine partials with the online softmax trick: maintain a running max m and running sum ℓ, and rescale the accumulated output when a new block raises the max. This gives an exact softmax without ever holding a full row.
  4. Never write the $n \times n$ matrix to HBM. In the backward pass, recompute it tile-by-tile from the saved m, ℓ — recomputation is cheaper than the memory traffic it avoids.

Result: attention memory drops from $O(n^2)$ to O(n), with 2–4× wall-clock speedup. This is the single technique most responsible for context windows going from 2k to 128k+. It is now the default path in F.scaled_dot_product_attention.

Online softmax, in two lines

Given a new block of scores with max $m'$ and exponent sum $\ell'$, merge with running $(m,\ell,o)$ via $m^{new}=\max(m,m')$ and

$$\ell^{new}=e^{m-m^{new}}\ell + e^{m'-m^{new}}\ell', \qquad o^{new}=\frac{e^{m-m^{new}}\ell\, o + e^{m'-m^{new}}\ell'\, o'}{\ell^{new}}$$

Exact, numerically stable, and streaming. Worth deriving once by hand.

Debugging checklist when your Transformer won't learn
  1. Loss stuck near $\log|V|$? That is the uniform-prediction loss (≈10.8 for V=50k). Your model is outputting noise — check the mask, the label shift, and whether the LR is far too high or low.
  2. Loss near zero within a few steps? Label leakage. You almost certainly forgot to shift targets by one, or your mask is not applied.
  3. Diverges after a few hundred steps? Post-LN without warmup, or missing gradient clipping.
  4. Works at L=2, breaks at L=24? Missing the 1/√(2L) residual init scaling, or Post-LN.
  5. Always sanity-check by overfitting one batch to near-zero loss first. If that fails, the bug is in the model, not the data pipeline.

Why decoder-only won
  1. Training signal density. Next-token prediction supervises every position. BERT-style MLM supervises only the ~15% of masked positions — roughly 6× less signal per token of data.
  2. No train/inference mismatch. MLM inserts [MASK] tokens that never appear downstream.
  3. Task unification. Any task becomes a string. No architectural change is needed for classification vs. translation vs. QA — this is what makes in-context learning and, later, agentic tool use possible.
  4. Serving efficiency. The KV cache (§12).

Encoder models are far from dead, though: for embedding and retrieval — the backbone of RAG in Module 9 — bidirectional encoders are still superior per parameter, because there a representation of the whole text is what you want.

§ 16

What heads actually learn

▸ Elhage et al. 2021 — Mathematical Framework ▸ Olsson et al. 2022 — Induction Heads ▸ Jain & Wallace 2019 — Attention is not Explanation

This section previews Module 8 (Interpretability) but belongs here, because it is what makes the Q/K/V split feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.

QK circuits and OV circuits

Every head factors into two independent low-rank operators:

$$\underbrace{W_{QK}=W_QW_K^\top}_{\text{where to read from}}\qquad \underbrace{W_{OV}=W_VW_O}_{\text{what to write}}$$

Only the products matter — the individual factorisation is not identifiable. Reading a head means answering two separate questions: which source positions does its QK circuit select? and what does its OV circuit copy into the destination?

Induction heads: the mechanism behind in-context learning

The most important discovered circuit. It needs exactly two layers and completes the pattern … [A][B] … [A] → [B]:

  1. Layer k: a previous-token head. At the position of B, it attends to A and writes "I was preceded by A" into B's residual stream.
  2. Layer k+1: the induction head. At the later occurrence of A, its query is "who was preceded by A?" — which matches the key that layer k just wrote at B. Its OV circuit then copies B to the output.

Olsson et al. showed that induction heads form abruptly during training, in a narrow window that coincides with a visible bump in the loss curve — and that this is exactly when few-shot in-context learning ability appears. The ability to learn from examples in the prompt is not a mysterious emergent property; it is a two-layer circuit you can point at.

Attention weights are not explanations

It is tempting to read an attention heatmap as "what the model used". Be careful. Jain & Wallace showed you can often find very different attention distributions that produce identical predictions. Attention weights ignore the magnitude of the value vectors, and they say nothing about the OV circuit or downstream MLPs. Attention maps are a hypothesis-generating tool, not evidence. Confirm with causal interventions — activation patching, ablation — which is the Module 8 method.

§ 17

A complete numeric trace

Work this by hand once and causal attention will never be mysterious again. Three tokens, one head, $d_k=d_v=2$.

$$Q=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\quad K=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\quad V=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&2\\3&1\end{bmatrix}$$

Step 1 — raw scores $S=QK^\top$

$$S=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\\1&1&2\end{bmatrix}$$

Step 2 — scale by $\sqrt{d_k}=\sqrt2\approx1.4142$

$$\tilde S=\begin{bmatrix}0.707&0&0.707\\0&0.707&0.707\\0.707&0.707&1.414\end{bmatrix}$$

Step 3 — apply the causal mask

$$\tilde S+M=\begin{bmatrix}0.707&-\infty&-\infty\\0&0.707&-\infty\\0.707&0.707&1.414\end{bmatrix}$$

Step 4 — row-wise softmax

Row 0: only one survivor, so the weight is forced to 1.0 — the first token always attends entirely to itself, in every layer, in every model.
Row 1: $e^{0}=1$, $e^{0.707}=2.028$, sum 3.028 → [0.330, 0.670, 0].
Row 2: $e^{0.707}=2.028$, $e^{0.707}=2.028$, $e^{1.414}=4.113$, sum 8.170 → [0.248, 0.248, 0.503].

$$A=\begin{bmatrix}1.000&0&0\\0.330&0.670&0\\0.248&0.248&0.503\end{bmatrix}$$

Step 5 — output $O=AV$

$$O=\begin{bmatrix}1.000&0.000\\0.330&1.340\\1.759&1.000\end{bmatrix}$$
The counterfactual that proves the mask matters

Without the mask, row 0 would softmax over [0.707, 0, 0.707] → [0.401, 0.198, 0.401], giving output [1.604, 0.797] instead of [1.000, 0.000]. Token 0's representation would carry information from tokens 1 and 2 — the very tokens it is supposed to predict. The loss would collapse toward zero and the model would learn nothing transferable. That single row is the entire argument for causal masking.

Check your understanding

Verify each row of A sums to 1. Then observe that columns do not: column 0 sums to 1.578 and column 2 to 0.503. Token 0 is read by everyone; token 2 is read only by itself. This "early tokens are over-read" effect is the origin of the attention sink phenomenon.

§ 18

Minimal decoder-only Transformer in PyTorch

▸ karpathy/nanoGPT ▸ karpathy/build-nanogpt

Everything in §5–§9, in about 70 lines. Read it alongside the equations.

import math, torch, torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class CausalSelfAttention(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d, n_head, block_size, dropout=0.0):
        super().__init__()
        assert d % n_head == 0
        self.n_head, self.d_k = n_head, d // n_head
        # one fused matmul for Q, K, V  ->  3d columns
        self.qkv  = nn.Linear(d, 3 * d, bias=False)
        self.proj = nn.Linear(d, d, bias=False)   # W_O
        self.drop = nn.Dropout(dropout)
        # lower-triangular causal mask, registered as a non-parameter buffer
        self.register_buffer("mask",
            torch.tril(torch.ones(block_size, block_size)).view(1, 1, block_size, block_size))

    def forward(self, x):
        B, n, d = x.shape
        q, k, v = self.qkv(x).split(d, dim=2)
        # (B, n, d) -> (B, n_head, n, d_k)  so the matmul batches over heads
        q = q.view(B, n, self.n_head, self.d_k).transpose(1, 2)
        k = k.view(B, n, self.n_head, self.d_k).transpose(1, 2)
        v = v.view(B, n, self.n_head, self.d_k).transpose(1, 2)

        att = (q @ k.transpose(-2, -1)) / math.sqrt(self.d_k)      # (B,h,n,n)
        att = att.masked_fill(self.mask[:, :, :n, :n] == 0,
                              torch.finfo(att.dtype).min)          # mask BEFORE softmax
        att = self.drop(F.softmax(att, dim=-1))
        y = att @ v                                                # (B,h,n,d_k)
        y = y.transpose(1, 2).contiguous().view(B, n, d)      # concat heads
        return self.proj(y)
        # In production, replace the 5 lines above with:
        #   y = F.scaled_dot_product_attention(q, k, v, is_causal=True)
        #   -> dispatches to FlashAttention: same maths, O(n) memory

class Block(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d, n_head, block_size):
        super().__init__()
        self.ln1, self.ln2 = nn.LayerNorm(d), nn.LayerNorm(d)
        self.attn = CausalSelfAttention(d, n_head, block_size)
        self.mlp  = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(d, 4*d), nn.GELU(), nn.Linear(4*d, d))

    def forward(self, x):
        x = x + self.attn(self.ln1(x))   # Pre-LN: residual path stays an identity
        x = x + self.mlp(self.ln2(x))
        return x

class GPT(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, V, d=384, n_layer=6, n_head=6, block_size=256):
        super().__init__()
        self.block_size = block_size
        self.wte = nn.Embedding(V, d)            # token embedding
        self.wpe = nn.Embedding(block_size, d)   # learned positions
        self.blocks = nn.ModuleList([Block(d, n_head, block_size) for _ in range(n_layer)])
        self.ln_f = nn.LayerNorm(d)
        self.head = nn.Linear(d, V, bias=False)
        self.head.weight = self.wte.weight       # weight tying

    def forward(self, idx, targets=None):
        B, n = idx.shape
        pos = torch.arange(n, device=idx.device)
        x = self.wte(idx) + self.wpe(pos)
        for blk in self.blocks: x = blk(x)
        logits = self.head(self.ln_f(x))         # (B, n, V)
        if targets is None: return logits, None
        loss = F.cross_entropy(logits.view(-1, logits.size(-1)), targets.view(-1))
        return logits, loss

    @torch.no_grad()
    def generate(self, idx, max_new_tokens, temperature=1.0, top_k=None):
        for _ in range(max_new_tokens):
            idx_cond = idx[:, -self.block_size:]          # crop to context
            logits, _ = self(idx_cond)
            logits = logits[:, -1, :] / temperature   # only the LAST position matters
            if top_k is not None:
                v, _ = torch.topk(logits, top_k)
                logits[logits < v[:, [-1]]] = -float('inf')
            nxt = torch.multinomial(F.softmax(logits, dim=-1), num_samples=1)
            idx = torch.cat((idx, nxt), dim=1)        # append and repeat
        return idx
Read the code for these five things
  1. qkv is one Linear of width 3d — Q, K, V are computed in a single matmul and split. Faster, identical maths.
  2. The view/transpose dance is all that multi-head attention is at the tensor level: split the width into h lanes, batch the matmul over them.
  3. masked_fill happens before softmax, using finfo.min rather than -1e9 so it is safe in fp16 and bf16 alike.
  4. Residual adds are outside the norms — Pre-LN.
  5. generate takes logits[:, -1, :] and throws away the other n−1 predictions. That waste is exactly what the KV cache eliminates.
§ 19

Misconceptions and pitfalls

Conceptual
  • "Attention weights show what the model is thinking." They show routing, not contribution. Value magnitude and the OV circuit matter just as much. See §16.
  • "Self-attention is quadratic, therefore Transformers are quadratic." Only for $n \gtrsim 6d$. Below that the MLPs dominate — which is why GPT-3 at n=2048 spent most of its FLOPs outside attention.
  • "Deeper always means more expressive per unit of compute." At fixed budget, depth and width trade off; scaling-law work finds broad optima but strongly penalises extreme aspect ratios.
  • "Positional encodings are concatenated." They are added (sinusoidal/learned) or applied as a rotation (RoPE). Concatenation would waste width.
  • "The causal mask makes the model unidirectional in a limiting way." Information still flows forward across layers, so a token at position 50 can be influenced by token 3 via any number of intermediate hops. What is forbidden is only future→past.
  • "Multi-head costs h× more than single-head." No — $d_k=d/h$ keeps FLOPs and parameters constant (§8).
Implementation
  • Forgetting to shift targets by one → loss collapses to ~0 → model is useless.
  • Applying the mask after softmax without renormalising → rows sum to <1.
  • Using -1e9 in fp16 → overflow to -inf → NaN when an entire row is masked. Use torch.finfo(dtype).min.
  • Padding without a padding mask → the model attends to <pad>. And if a row is fully masked, softmax produces NaN — guard it.
  • Scaling by √d_model instead of √d_k. It is the head dimension.
  • Leaving dropout on during evaluation (model.eval()).
  • Applying RoPE to V. RoPE goes on Q and K only — it is a score-space transformation.
§ 20

Exam-style questions

Q1 · Derivation

Derive the variance of $q^\top k$ and explain why the softmax gradient vanishes without the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling.

A. See §6: $\operatorname{Var}=d_k$ under unit-variance independent components; large scores saturate softmax; the Jacobian $\alpha_i(\delta_{ij}-\alpha_j)$ vanishes at one-hot, so no gradient reaches $W_Q,W_K$ and the routing never learns.

Q2 · Reasoning

A student proposes removing the causal mask during training and adding it only at inference, arguing training will be "richer". What happens?

A. Training loss collapses to near zero because position t can read $x_{t+1}$ and copy it. Nothing predictive is learned, and at inference — where the future genuinely is unavailable — the model performs at chance. This is label leakage, and it is the single most instructive bug in the topic.

Q3 · Computation

A model has d=4096, h=32, L=32, V=32000, d_ff=11008, RoPE. Give (a) parameters, (b) KV cache at n=8192, fp16, batch 4.

A. (a) Attention $4d^2 = 67.1$M/layer; FFN with SwiGLU $3\,d\,d_{ff}=135.3$M/layer; total per layer 202.4M × 32 = 6.48B, plus embedding+unembedding $2\times32000\times4096=0.26$B → ≈ 6.7B. (b) $2\times32\times8192\times32\times128\times2\times4$ = 17.2 GB. Then explain how GQA with g=8 cuts this to 2.1 GB.

Q4 · Design

You must serve a 34B model at 128k context on one 80 GB GPU. Which three changes do you make first, and what does each buy?

A. (i) FlashAttention — removes the $O(n^2)$ activation matrix, exact, no quality cost. (ii) GQA — cuts KV cache by the group factor; requires uptraining if the checkpoint is MHA. (iii) KV-cache quantisation to int8/fp8 plus PagedAttention — halves cache bytes and eliminates fragmentation. Mention sliding-window attention as a fourth lever if some long-range recall can be traded away.

Q5 · Conceptual

Why can a decoder-only model cache K and V while an encoder cannot?

A. Causality: $k_j,v_j$ depend only on $x_{\le j}$, so appending a token cannot invalidate them. In a bidirectional encoder every representation depends on the full sequence, so the entire cache would be stale after each append.

Q6 · Analysis

Show that MHA equals a sum of per-head contributions, and say why this matters.

A. Block-partition $W_O$; see §8. It matters because heads are independent additive writers into the residual stream, which makes per-head ablation, head pruning, and circuit analysis well-defined operations.

Q7 · Positional

Prove RoPE makes attention scores depend only on relative position.

A. $(R_mq)^\top(R_nk)=q^\top R_m^\top R_nk=q^\top R_{n-m}k$, using orthogonality $R_m^\top=R_m^{-1}=R_{-m}$ and the group property $R_aR_b=R_{a+b}$ of 2-D rotations. Then note this holds blockwise over all $d_h/2$ frequency pairs.

Q8 · Diagnostic

Your 24-layer model's loss plateaus at 10.8 with V=50257. Diagnose.

A. $\ln 50257 = 10.82$ — the model is predicting uniformly, i.e. learning nothing. Ordered checks: is the LR sane (not 1e-1, not 1e-8)? Are targets shifted? Is the mask correct? Post-LN without warmup? Missing 1/√(2L) residual init? Then try to overfit a single batch — if that fails, the bug is in the model.
